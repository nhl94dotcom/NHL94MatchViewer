    Getting started

    The NHL '94 Viewer records games of NHL '94 played in RetroArch (Sega Genesis with the Genesis Plus GX core, Super Nintendo with the Snes9x core), then replays them from above with the game's own players, plus stats the game never shows.

    Recording games

    1. Start the helper: NHL94 Helper.exe (or NHL94 Helper.bat if you have Python). Leave its window open while you play.
    2. Start RetroArch and load NHL '94. The helper notices on its own and waits for a game.
    3. Play. Each game is recorded from the opening faceoff to the final horn, offline or on netplay. Pauses and instant replays are left out.
    4. About 3 seconds after the horn the game is saved and appears in your games list at http://localhost:9494. Click Watch.

    In netplay the helper records both teams, so one recording per game is enough. A game where the helper started late, or that stopped before the horn (a dropped connection), is still saved, but counts as "part of a game".

    Where things are

    The games list (Games) has every recording: Watch, Make game file (a single page to send to someone), Delete. Season stats adds games up. The .exe keeps everything in Documents\NHL94 Viewer.

    Watching a game

    Playback

    Play / pause
    The Play button or the space bar.
    Jump
    Drag the bar, or the arrow keys: 1 second (5 with Shift).
    Speed
    0.25x to 4x, beside the bar.

    The rink

    Move and zoom
    Drag to move, the mouse wheel to zoom (or + and -). On a touchscreen, drag to move and use + and - on the toolbar.
    Turn
    R turns 90 degrees (Shift+R the other way), Shift + wheel turns a little; the toolbar on the ice does the same.
    Reset
    0, double-click the ice, or Reset.
    Follow the puck
    F, or in the View menu: a TV-style camera that keeps the puck in view.

    The lineups

    Each side lists the players on the ice: position, number, name, goals and assists so far (goalies: saves and shots), and a thin energy bar. A yellow edge marks the skater a person is controlling; the highlighted row has the puck. Below, the controller lights up the buttons that player is pressing. Point at a player (tap on a phone) for his card: this game's numbers, skating, and his ratings.

    Team stats and scoring

    The scoring summary is on the left, the team stats on the right, both as of the moment the replay is at. Click a group's heading (Shooting, Advanced, Puck control ...) to open or close it; your choice is remembered.

    The View menu (⚙ View)

    Game sprites
    The game's own player pictures; off draws numbered discs.
    Event tags
    The pop-ups for what players do (Pass, Poke check, Failed one-timer ...), breakaways and posts. The player labels stay either way.
    Overhead rink
    The rink redrawn as if seen from straight above (off: the game's own leaning picture).
    Follow the puck
    As F.
    Sound
    The game's own sampled sounds (passes, shots, checks, the crowd). Sound settings sets their speed and volume.
    Live knowledge
    Shows only what could be known at each moment, as a live broadcast would: a shot's result waits until it is settled, for example.

    On a phone

    The rink is on top; below it, tabs switch between the lineups, the team stats and the scoring summary. The controls stay at the bottom.

    Maps and game flow

    The Map menu draws on the ice, building up as the replay plays; pick both teams, one team or one player beside it. Teams change ends between periods, so each team is kept attacking the end it attacks right now. Point at a marker for what it was; click it to jump there.

    Shot map
    Every shot attempt: goals (accent ring), saves (white ring), posts (diamond), wide (hollow), blocked (crossed). Squares are pass shots.
    Heat map
    Skating: where the skaters (or one player) went, puck or not. With the puck: where the puck was while they had it. Filters: period, strength (even, power play, shorthanded) and, for skating, attacking / defending / loose puck. With both teams it shows the difference: each spot in the colour of the team that spent more time there. Time is game time: nothing counts while the clock is stopped.
    Turnovers
    Giveaways (hollow triangles, in the colour of the team that lost it) and takeaways (filled).
    Passes
    Every pass and how it ended.
    Zone entries
    Where the puck crossed the blue line: carried (disc), passed (diamond), dumped (hollow); an accent ring means a shot attempt came of it.
    Goalie report
    Every shot a goalie faced, with save rates by distance and type, human or computer in net, and what happened after each save.
    Game flow
    A strip over the controls: each team's shot attempts piling up over the game, goals as dots, power plays shaded. Click it to jump there.

    Highlights, clips and game files

    Highlights
    The game's three stars, and its highlights (goals, big saves, posts, big hits, breakaways that came to a shot), each from 5 seconds before to 2 after. Click one to watch it, Play all to see them in turn, or Save as video.
    Clip
    Saves any stretch (up to a minute; or pick a goal) as a video or an animated GIF, at normal or half speed, as the ice looks now (angle, zoom, follow, map).
    Save game file
    One page with this game, the viewer, the rink, sprites and sounds inside (about 5 MB). It opens in any browser, phones included, by itself: send it to your opponent.

    Season stats

    The season page adds up any set of games. Make a season (New season: a name, the ROM, your team, when it starts and, if you like, ends): its games are the whole games played on that ROM from its start until it ends or the next season on that ROM starts. Several seasons can share a ROM. Untick a game on the Games tab to leave it out.

    Standings
    Wins, losses, OT losses, ties, points (2 a win, 1 an OT loss or tie), goals, power play and penalty kill; your team's record, home and away, last five.
    Skaters, Goalies, Team stats, Head to head
    The usual totals. Goalies also split save % by a person or the computer in net, high-danger save %, rebound rate, and show where they get beaten.
    Advanced
    Corsi, Fenwick, PDO, high-danger chances, rebounds, EA Specials, shot assists, costly turnovers, zone entries. On-ice stats are for leagues with line changes.
    Play style
    How chances were made, the person against the computer, each person's controller habits, and the passing network.
    Heat maps
    A team's or skater's heat map over the whole season.

    Glossary of stats

    Tip: point at any stat in the team stats or on a player's card, or at a column heading on the season page, for a short explanation of it.

    Times are game-clock time (the clock you see in the game, which runs about 2.5 times as fast as real time and stops at whistles) everywhere except skating speed and distance and the controller's presses a minute, which use real time.

    Basics

    G, A, PTS
    Goals, assists, points (goals + assists), as the game credits them.
    +/-
    Goals for minus goals against while the player was on the ice, as the NHL counts it: power-play and penalty-shot goals count for neither side.
    SOG
    Shots on goal, as the game counts them. The game does not count every stopped shot (never a pass shot unless it scores).
    S% (shooting %)
    Goals divided by shots on goal.
    PPG, SHG, GWG
    Power-play, shorthanded and game-winning goals (the goal that put the winner ahead of the loser's final total).
    PIM
    Penalty minutes.
    TOI
    Ice time, in game minutes.
    SA, GA, SV%
    Shots and goals against a goalie; save % = saves / shots against.
    GA/GP, SO
    Goals against a game; shutouts.
    PP%, PK%
    Power-play goals / power plays; penalties killed / times shorthanded.

    Shots

    Shot attempt (iCF)
    Every shot, whether on goal, wide or blocked.
    Wide, blocked
    A miss; blocked when an opposing skater (not the goalie) got it or it changed course off one.
    Saved, not counted
    The goalie stopped it but the game did not count a shot on goal.
    Wrist shot, slapshot
    Told apart by how long the shot was wound up (0.8 seconds or more is a slapshot).
    One-timer
    A shot straight off a pass, without stopping the puck. A failed one-timer is a swing at a pass that never arrived.
    Pass shot
    A pass (the B button) used as a shot.
    Deflection, scramble
    A counted shot with no shooting motion: a redirect beside the net, or a scramble.
    High danger (HD)
    An attempt from the slot: within about 20 ft of the net and not wide of it. A third of these go in, against about one in seven elsewhere.
    Rebound
    An attempt by the same team within 3 seconds of a save that gave them the puck back.
    Wraparound
    A shot from beside the post just after carrying the puck behind the net.
    Post
    Off the post (the game counts it as a shot on goal).

    The puck and the play

    Possession
    Game seconds each team controlled the puck. A loose puck counts for the team that had it if they get it back, otherwise as transition. Split by zone: attacking, neutral, defensive.
    Attack zone
    The game's own count: time the puck spent in that team's attacking zone, whoever had it.
    Zone entry
    The puck crossing the blue line into the attacking zone: carried, passed, or dumped in; and whether a shot attempt came of it before it left the zone.
    Off the rush, sustained, off a faceoff
    Where an attempt came from: within 5 seconds of the zone entry (rush), later in that stay in the zone (sustained pressure), or from a faceoff in that zone.
    Odd-man rush
    A carried or passed zone entry with more attackers than defenders back (at least two attackers): 2-on-1, 3-on-2 ...
    Breakaway
    As the game counts it: the carrier crossing the blue line ahead of every defender.
    Giveaway, takeaway
    The puck changing teams without a whistle: a takeaway when the other team forced it (poke check, hook, check, or stolen), otherwise a giveaway (a pass picked off, the puck lost).
    Costly giveaway, turnovers → shot against
    Giveaways (turnovers) the other team turned into a shot attempt within 5 seconds.
    Shot assist, attempts off a pass
    A pass that led straight to a teammate's shot attempt.
    Passing network
    Completed passes between each pair of teammates, and the shot attempts off them.
    Passes, pass %
    Passes the game counted and completed (one-timer passes count as completed).
    Faceoffs
    Won, and by zone.
    Icing
    Touch-up icing: shot from a team's own half across the far goal line untouched.

    Physical

    Checks, hits taken
    Body checks that landed, thrown and taken.
    Big hit
    A check that upended a player or sent him into the boards or the bench.
    Bumped off the puck, pucks lost on contact
    The puck carrier lost the puck while an opposing skater was up against him, with no check landed, no poke check or hook, and without going down: the two only rubbed into each other. The card shows each player's "Bumped off puck" (he lost it) and "Bumped puck loose" (he was the one in contact). Team stats count them, and how many went straight to the other team.
    Attack-zone possession per goal
    A team's possession in its attacking zone (game time) divided by its goals: how long it needed the puck in the other end for each goal (lower is more dangerous). On the season page also the other way round: the opponents' attacking-zone possession for each goal the team allowed (higher is better defence). One game's number is the same seen from either side; over a season the two tell offence and defence apart.
    Carried by D / C / W
    Who carries the puck: the share of a team's time with the puck on a stick that the carrier was a defenceman, the centre or a winger, by the position he was playing then. Two defencemen, two wingers and one centre, so equal use would be 40 / 20 / 40. In the team stats (Puck control) and, across games, on the season page's Play style tab ("Who carries the puck", by zone, with carried zone entries by position).
    Pass direction and zones
    Each pass is classed by the way the puck left the stick, against the way the passer's team attacks: forward (within 30° of straight up the ice), diagonal forward (30–60°), sideways (within 30° of straight across, so a little forward or back still counts), diagonal back, backward; and by the zone it was made in and the zone it reached (attacking, neutral, defensive, in the passer's team's terms). Shown in the team stats ("Passing by zone and direction"), on the card, and on the season page's Play style tab ("Passing by zone"), with each player's game time with the puck in that zone and passes per minute of it: high means he moves the puck on, low means he skates with it.
    Where a goal went in
    Each goal in the scoring summary and its banner has a small picture of the net, seen from the shooter's side, with the spot where the puck crossed the goal line (posts at the sides, the crossbar at the top). (Older SNES recordings converted before the puck's height was found show a dashed line across the net instead.)
    Where a shot was aimed
    Both games aim every shot at one of nine points in the net, picked by the direction the stick is held as you shoot, by screen direction: up, up-left or up-right aims high; left, right or no direction middle height; down-left, down or down-right along the ice; left and right are the screen's sides (so shooting down the screen, the stick's left is the shooter's right). The game then scatters the shot at random (more for hard shots, weak shooters and long range). Shot tooltips say where each was aimed; on a goal's net picture the blue ring is the aim and the yellow dot where it went in; the card and the season page count where each player aims.
    Shot speed
    How hard a shot left the stick, in mph at the same scale as skating speeds: the puck's speed from the game's own speed words, which match its movement at about 0.015 game units a second per speed unit. In shot tooltips, on the card (average and hardest) and on the season page.
    Checks that pay off, goal off a check
    A check after which the checking team had the puck within 2 seconds (before the other team got it back); then a shot attempt within 5 seconds of that; or a goal within 8 seconds of the check, when the check was on the puck carrier and the other team never had the puck between: that goal is tagged "off a check".
    Goalie giveaway
    A goal straight off a goalie's bad pass: his pass was picked off and the other team scored within 6 seconds, directly or with up to two passes, without his team getting the puck back. The goal is tagged "goalie giveaway"; it shows on the goalie's card and the season page.
    Net knocked off
    A skater (or goalie) bumped a net off its spot and play stopped. An attacker knocking the other team's net off gets the faceoff moved out to a dot just outside that zone; a defender knocking his own off keeps it in the zone. The faceoff that follows tells which it was; whoever was nearest the net on that side gets the credit. Shown as a banner, in the team stats (theirs / own), on the card and on the season page. Much more common on the SNES.
    Bobbled pass
    A pass that reached a teammate close enough to take it, but bounced off him instead of onto his stick. Not counted when he swung at it (a one-timer) or passed it on, when an opponent picked it off, or when the pass missed and came off the boards. It still counts if he gets the puck back afterwards. Shown as a "Bobbled" tag, on the receiver's card and in the team stats.
    Lost it on the goalie, pucks lost on a goalie
    The puck carrier skated into a goalie, his own or the other team's, and the puck came loose (the game knocks it off his stick). Counted apart from bumps by skaters, on the card ("Lost puck on goalie") and in the team stats.
    Injury
    A player hurt, with the check that caused it, and whether the game put him out for the rest of the period or of the game (read from the game itself: the computer may keep a player on the bench even after he is fit again).

    Advanced

    Corsi (CF, CA, CF%)
    Shot attempts for and against (on goal, wide or blocked), and a team's share of them: the most used measure of who controlled play.
    Fenwick (FF, FA, FF%)
    The same without blocked attempts.
    PDO
    Shooting % + save %, times 100. About 100 is normal; well above or below over a few games usually means luck.
    Even strength
    When both teams have the same number of players in the penalty box.
    HDCF, HDCA, HDCF%
    High-danger attempts for, against, and the share.
    Att. share, Pt. share
    A skater's share of his team's shot attempts, and of its goals (scored or assisted), in the games he played.
    On goal %
    A skater's attempts that were shots on goal.
    Reb%
    A goalie's saves that gave the shooters a rebound.
    On-ice stats, CF% rel, zone starts, OZS%
    For leagues with line changes: Corsi while a skater was on the ice, against his team's while he was on the bench (rel), and the faceoffs he started in each zone. Without line changes they are just the team's numbers.

    The person and the computer

    Person / CPU
    Whether the skater was the one a person was controlling at that moment. The person always has the puck carrier, so checks and penalties are where the computer's skaters show.
    Person in net, CPU in net
    Whether a person had taken control of the goalie when the shot was released.
    Presses a minute
    A, B and C presses per minute of live play.
    Speed bursts / check tries
    C without the puck: the game uses it for both.
    Poke check, hook → takeaway
    Poke checks and hooks by the person's skater, and how many took the puck.
    Goalie time, dives
    Time a person controlled the goalie, and the dives.

    Game score and three stars

    Skaters: 0.75 a goal, 0.7 a first assist, 0.55 a second, 0.075 a shot on goal, 0.05 a hit, 0.1 a takeaway, -0.1 a giveaway, -0.15 a penalty, 0.05 per shot attempt for (-0.05 against) and 0.15 per goal for (-0.15 against) while on the ice. Goalies: 0.2 a save, -0.5 a goal against, 1 for a shutout. The three best are the stars.

    Skating

    Skated, top speed
    Distance covered (miles) and the fastest half-second, on the game's own scale.

    Community terms

    EA Special
    Named after the player known as EA who invented it (not Electronic Arts). Genesis only: a person's skater flips a forehand wrist shot from his forehand side, close in and off to the side, at a goalie in position; the goalie bobbles it and is pushed back, and the shooter or a teammate puts the rebound in within 2 seconds. An EA Special attempt is the flip itself, whatever came of it (saved, blocked, wide; not a goal straight from the shot), after carrying the puck (not in a scramble), by a skater coming in from the side (not skating back toward his own end or drifting out toward the boards); it counts as an EA Special when his team scores within 2 seconds without a defender having had the puck. The goalie often dives after it, as if to cover a puck he is nowhere near.
    Pass shot
    Using the pass button to shoot: the puck goes off quickly with no target. The game does not count it as a shot on goal unless it scores.

    Troubleshooting

    The games page will not open (localhost)
    The helper is not running: start it and leave its window open, then reload.
    Windows warns about the helper
    It is an unsigned program that reads RetroArch's memory (read only). Choose "More info", then "Run anyway".
    A game is missing
    The helper has to be running at the opening faceoff and until the horn. A recording under 45 seconds of play at 0-0 is thrown away.
    A game is not in the season
    Seasons count whole games only (from the opening faceoff to the horn) on the season's ROM between its dates; check the Games tab and "Whole games only".
    Numbers differ from the game's stats screen
    Goals, assists, shots on goal and checks are the game's own; attempts, possession and the rest are worked out from the replay.
